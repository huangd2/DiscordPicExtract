## Key Findings

### 1. Exit Strategy Impact
- **Even thirds (33%/33%/33%) outperforms 50%/25%/25%**
- Strategy 4.1 achieves 9.76% vs Strategy 4's 8.74% (+1.02%)
- More balanced position reduction improves profit capture

### 2. Stop-Loss Effectiveness
- Higher stop-loss frequency doesn't necessarily mean worse performance
- Strategy 4.1 has 30% stop-loss rate but best average loss control (-$19.37)
- Stop-loss prevents catastrophic losses effectively

### 3. Trade Quality vs Quantity
- Strategy 4.1 has fewer trades (100) but better average P&L ($9.76)
- Quality over quantity approach yields better results
- Fewer trades can mean more selective entries

### 4. Cross-Day Performance
- Strategy 2 has perfect cross-day performance but no stop-loss
- Strategy 4 had negative cross-day P&L (-$268.13) - concerning
- Strategy 4.1 improves cross-day P&L to positive ($30.15)

### 5. Risk-Return Trade-off
- Strategy 2: Low risk (no stop-loss needed), good return (9.06%)
- Strategy 3: Moderate risk, lower return (7.12%)
- Strategy 4: Moderate risk, good return (8.74%)
- Strategy 4.1: Moderate risk, **best return (9.76%)**

## Conclusion

**Strategy 4.1 emerges as the clear winner** with the highest return (9.76%) while maintaining excellent risk management characteristics.
